    A2CFlow: Range-Aware Rectified Flow for Efficient Adverse-Weather LiDAR Restoration

    Published in Revised and resubmitted to IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L), 2026

    Adverse weather degrades LiDAR measurements nonuniformly, leaving distant regions and missing-return locations weakly constrained by the corrupted observation. Effective restoration must therefore suppress weather-induced clutter while reconstructing clean geometry that is poorly observed or absent. We introduce Adverse-to-Clear Flow (A2CFlow), a conditional rectified-flow framework that generates a clean range-and-reflectance scan from Gaussian noise conditioned on a single adverse-weather observation. To account for spatially varying restoration difficulty, we propose Range-Aware Flow Scheduling (RAFS), a physics-informed reparameterization derived from observed range and return validity. RAFS assigns location-dependent flow times, providing difficult regions with cleaner training states and greater early sampling progress without changing the transport endpoints or increasing network evaluations. We further compress the transport into an efficient few-step distilled model that retains the benefits of range-aware scheduling. Extensive experiments demonstrate improved restoration fidelity and downstream perception on Adverse-KITTI, as well as zero-shot transfer to real adverse-weather scans, while retaining efficient inference.

    Recommended citation: Peiyang Ni, Xiaorui Sun, Lu Zhang, Heng Tao Shen, Xiaofeng Zhu, and Ping Hu. "A2CFlow: Range-Aware Rectified Flow for Efficient Adverse-Weather LiDAR Restoration." Revised and resubmitted to IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L), 2026.

    Structure-to-Intensity Diffusion for Adverse-Weather LiDAR Generation

    Published in Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2026

    Adverse-weather LiDAR point cloud generation is challenged by complex weather-induced degradations. These degradations affect geometry and reflectance in fundamentally different ways, making joint modeling difficult and ambiguous, especially when diverse real-world training data is limited. To address this, we propose Structure-to-Intensity Diffusion (SiD), a diffusion-based framework that explicitly factorizes the denoising process at each time step: it first reconstructs the geometric structure, then conditions reflectance intensity denoising on the estimated structure. This structure-conditioned design decomposes the joint distribution, reduces modeling ambiguity, and leads to point clouds that are both geometrically coherent and radiometrically realistic. To mitigate data scarcity, we introduce Real-Prior Weather Simulation (RPWS), a degradation module that leverages real-world sensor statistics to synthesize physically plausible adverse-weather point clouds from clear scans. Extensive experiments demonstrate that, with similar model complexity, our approach outperforms the previous state-of-the-art in generating adverse-weather LiDAR scans with both structural and radiometric properties more closely aligned with real-world data.

    Recommended citation: Peiyang Ni, et al. (2026). "Structure-to-Intensity Diffusion for Adverse-Weather LiDAR Generation." In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR).
